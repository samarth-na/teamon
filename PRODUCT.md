# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Individual knowledge workers managing their own tasks. They're in flow — coding, writing, designing — and need to capture, organize, and triage work without leaving their context. They use the tool in short bursts (checking what's next, moving a card) and longer sessions (planning, reviewing). Ambient light varies: bright office during the day, dim room at night. They switch views fluidly depending on the question: "what must I do today" (list), "where is this stuck" (kanban), "when is this due" (calendar), "how long will this take" (timeline).

## Product Purpose

A personal task manager that gives the user four lenses on their work — list, kanban, calendar, timeline — so they can answer any question about their tasks without switching tools. Success is when the user captures a thought and finds it again in the right view, fast. The tool should feel like an extension of their working memory, not a database they have to maintain.

## Brand Personality

Clean, calm, focused. The tool recedes into the task. It's confident without being loud — Linear's precision meets Notion's warmth. No personality injected for personality's sake. The delight is in speed, clarity, and the feeling that the tool understands what you meant.

## Anti-references

- Jira and bloated PM tools — too many fields, too much chrome, feels like filling out forms
- Over-decorated SaaS dashboards with gradient cards and hero metrics
- "Fun" productivity tools that inject gamification or personality where none is needed
- Any interface where the chrome competes with the content for attention

## Design Principles

1. **Speed over spectacle** — Every animation, every click, every transition serves the user's flow or it doesn't exist. No decorative motion.
2. **Consistency is the affordance** — Same button shape, same form vocabulary, same icon style across all four views. The user should predict what things do before they interact.
3. **Content first, chrome second** — The tasks are the interface. Navigation, headers, and framing exist only to get out of the way.
4. **Calm over stimulation** — The tool should feel restful to look at. Low-contrast surfaces, restrained color, no blinking or pulsing.
5. **One action, one way** — Creating a task, changing its status, assigning a project — each action has exactly one interaction pattern across all views.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Keyboard-navigable throughout (Tab, Enter, Escape, shortcuts)
- Focus indicators visible on all interactive elements
- Respects `prefers-reduced-motion` — all animations disabled or reduced
- Touch targets minimum 44x44px on all interactive elements
- Color is never the sole indicator of meaning (priority, status have icons + text)
